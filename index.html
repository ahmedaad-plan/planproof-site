Frequently asked questions

What is a business continuity tabletop exercise?

A tabletop exercise is a facilitated, discussion-based session in which your team works through a realistic disruption scenario. Timed injects — new developments such as a system outage, a media inquiry or a regulatory question — test how your plans, roles and decisions hold up, without disrupting live operations.

Do your exercises support ISO 22301 and NCEMA 7000:2021?

Yes. Both standards expect organizations to exercise and test their business continuity arrangements. Our scenarios are designed around those requirements, and the debrief report records findings and actions you can use as evidence of exercising. An exercise supports compliance; it does not by itself certify it.

How long does an exercise take?

Most exercises run 60, 90 or 120 minutes, including a structured debrief at the end. You choose the duration when you submit your request, and the number and pace of injects is designed to fit it.

Who should take part?

The people who would make or carry out decisions in a real incident — typically executive leadership, operations, IT, communications, HR, legal and compliance, plus any department central to the scenario. You select the participating departments in the request form.

What is the difference between the self-service kit and a facilitated session?

The self-service kit is a complete, ready-to-run exercise package your own team runs internally. In a facilitated session, a specialist runs the live exercise with your team and then delivers a written debrief report identifying real gaps in your plan.

What do we receive at the end?

A debrief report summarizing what was tested, key findings, and concrete recommendations tied to what actually happened in the exercise.

Is the information we share kept confidential?

Yes. Details and plans are reviewed directly, one-to-one, and are never published. Please remove information that isn't essential, such as staff names or exact financial figures. A mutual NDA is available on request.
